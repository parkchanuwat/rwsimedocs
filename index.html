Why IME bugs happen

An input method editor sits between the keyboard and the text field. The app sees keys that are not yet text. Most IME bugs come from the app treating those in-between keys as if they were final.

The window on the right replays real IME sessions. Blue text is the draft: the IME owns it until it is committed.

Six bug families to test for

Each card links to the Bug Lab tests that reproduce it.

Space→▶

Hotkey leak

A single-key shortcut such as Space or 1 fires while the IME is using that key to pick a candidate.

Enter→Sent early

Enter submits early

The Enter that confirms a conversion also sends the message or presses OK, or leaves a character behind.

１２０≠120

Full-width leakage

The IME produces ： １２０ or curly quotes where the field only accepts ASCII.

한글→ㅎㅏㄴ

Lost composition

Moving focus, or re-rendering the field while you type, throws away or breaks the draft text.

Candidate window position

The list opens at a corner or far from the caret in custom-drawn, scaled or scrolled text fields. Checked by eye.

ㅠ≠b

Key code vs character

Shortcuts matched by typed character fail when Hangul, Zhuyin or kana mode changes what the key reports.

Everything here describes common behavior. Exact symptoms depend on the OS version, IME version, settings and the app's text engine. Treat each example as a pattern to try on your build and confirm before filing.

Hands-on · your real keyboard

Bug Lab

Each test is a small demo app with one bug pattern. Type with your own IME and follow the steps. The bench reads the real browser events and tells you whether the bug showed up.

Your computer

The labs assume a standard US QWERTY keyboard. A JIS or Korean keyboard works too: its extra keys (半角/全角, かな, 한/영) do the same job as the switch keys listed. Function keys and Ctrl or Cmd shortcuts need a hardware keyboard.
